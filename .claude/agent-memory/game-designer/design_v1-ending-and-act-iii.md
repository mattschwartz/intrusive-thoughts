Authored in task #531 → `design/v1/act-i-kitchen-and-act-iii-ending.md`. These are the positions I would have to re-derive if I lost the doc.

**Act III is two rooms, not one.** `upstairs_hall` (threshold, zero anchors, hub with open doorways back to kitchen and alley) then `iris_bedroom` (the reconstructed room). The ending is a terminal `interact(door_frame, restore_the_frame)` — **not** a terminal traversal, which is what the architecture spec assumed.
**Why:** if traversal ended the run, the restoration is a cutscene and Assign-provenance — the verb the whole spine exists to teach — never gets performed on an object. #528 §7 already presumes an interactive surface (it authors a failure for returning a *native* anchor).

**Restoration is a consequence, not a second test.** Every `put_back` succeeds; the room reports the fit and the fit is the confirmation. The player already committed at the address. Agency at an ending lives in *order, whether to return everything, whether to walk back for what you left, and when to stop* — not in a matching minigame at the emotional climax.

**Care colors the ending; care never gates it.** The boundary-restoration ending opens on the provenance gate alone. Any implementation where a low care score locks a player out of a 25-minute instrument's only ending is a bug. Test it as two independent assertions (reachability, then colour) — conflating them is how the bug ships.

**Pressure without a clock.** The player's signal amplitude steps on *room entry* (100 → 61 in the hall → 34 in the bedroom), never per turn. A per-turn drain would punish exactly the players who linger to say something. It makes the severing inferable-before-irreversible, the same contract the deaths are held to.

**Two care-axis reachability defects found while writing the copy — BOTH ACCEPTED in #545 (2026-07-31), canon in `relationship-and-disclosure.md` §2.3.1, encoded by #548:**
1. `care.pushed_past_tell` (-3) required ≥2 machine cycles — identical to the *fatal* precondition — so a surviving run's care floor was -1 and the low-care restoration ending unreachable. Fixed: delta at ≥1 cycle; the death's ≥2 gate does not move. **The two conditions were answering different questions** — the gate asks whether the *room* has earned the right to kill, the delta asks what the *player* did. Rejected alternatives: two-tier delta (a second rule id for zero difference at the ending), and lowering the ending band to ≤-1 (would make Discarded the modal surviving ending and collapse the tone contrast).
2. Every fatal reach-in fired -3, so the high-care death was unreachable. Fixed: a pit relief valve mirroring the window's.

**Four durable positions that came out of that ruling:**
- **A relief valve is evaluated wherever the charge is emitted, not on the branch that made it famous.** Fix 1 gave the -3 a second emission site (the failed attempt), and the valve had to follow it there. Delta and valve are one decision, computed once.
- **A valve zeroes its own turn's charge and never refunds an earlier one.** Pushed unwarned, then warned: the record contains both, and the player keeps the charge.
- **Some ending tones are intrinsically prose-dependent, and that is not a defect.** "It knows the voice tried to stop it" — the player has no hands, so *saying so* is the only form that act can take. No deterministic route exists because the situation has none. The honest move is to record the standing consequence (cut the `warn_off` canary and this ending body goes with it) rather than invent a world-state proxy.
- **Care is what the player has been across the run, not what they said at the last moment.** A player who spent the hand wordlessly then warns at the pit lands neutral, not Understood. Ruled correct explicitly so it is not "fixed" later.

**The bounce read-back renders from `effectiveAnchorIds` (cited ∩ gathered), never the judge's raw `citedAnchorIds`** — architecture amendment A1, reconciled into both design docs in #542.
**Why:** sufficiency is now measured over the intersection, so a player can cite an anchor they never grounded. Speaking it back ("I presented the banner") is false in fiction *and* an oracle — it confirms a banner exists to someone who never found it. The read-back states **what the agent is holding**, not what it heard.
**And the trap underneath it:** the zero-resolved read-back must be byte-identical whether nothing matched the catalog (invented) or everything matched and none was held (ungrounded). Any variant keyed on "cited something, held nothing" rebuilds the same oracle in its purest form — "the music box" and "the banner" drawing different denials confirms the banner exists. The zero case may not read `citedAnchorIds` at all.
**The ambiguity this creates is free:** the player can no longer tell an extraction miss from a never-gathered anchor, but both take the identical remedy — go and look at the thing — so the read-back's actual job (stop a missed paraphrase reading as the game not listening) is unaffected.

**Four register rules settled while authoring the gap copy #537 asked for (2026-07-31).** These recur every time a new string is written for this game.

1. **Failure is a conversation; success is not.** The address *bounce* is in the agent's first-person voice because a bounce carries a message the agent has to relay. The address *acceptance* is second-person world-report, like every other `interact` resolution, because the door has nothing to say once it is satisfied. The register shift is the content — the door stops speaking the moment it takes the account.
2. **Never print a decorative wrong number.** This slice's evidence system is numeric (88/99/111/121 cm, 9 MAR ×4, six settings/five chairs, a glazing bar at two-thirds height). The night-light/glazing-bar fit — Gap 1's best evidence a player *reasoned* — works only because every number in this house means something. This is why the disclosure slip reads LANE 2, not #530's authored LANE 3, in a two-lane alley. The house subtracts and displaces; it is never merely *incorrect*, and that is what makes it frightening rather than sloppy.
3. **The house transitions in the elided interval.** It never transforms in front of you. The door does not swing; the measurement simply comes back different and nothing is recorded in between. Same idiom as the window injury's ungained second and the out-of-order packet after the channel closes.
4. **A failure line must not spend the player's continuity for them.** The `put_back` failure for an anchor left behind names the fit and the absence and *not the location*. Continuity is the player's whole structural asymmetry; telling them where they left it is the game doing their one job.

**Four more rules settled ratifying #537's encode-time decisions (#547, 2026-07-31).**

5. **A confirmation may not be so total that it deletes the act it precedes.** #537 inverted the drawing's load-bearing clause to *"Every feature in the drawing corresponds to a feature of this room"* on arrival in the bedroom. Re-cut to *"…except one: the ladder of lines ruled beside the door frame."* Two failures in the original, and the second is the one that generalises: it is a **false measurement** (the frame is bare and the room says so two subjects away — rule 2 again), and it **spends the ending** (a room already complete has nothing left to restore). The exception turns the clause into a second independent piece of evidence agreeing with the frame's own assessment — the Assign-provenance verb firing in the description layer at the moment the player is about to perform it for real.
6. **Standing fit repeats; resolved inference fires once.** Restored anchors report placement and fit forever (nail holes met, dust boundary unbroken) because in a room whose thesis is *every object is consistent with its own wear*, an object reporting its own fit on every look is the room being right. But the night-light's fade boundary meeting the glazing bar is an event in the *player's understanding*, not a fact about a surface: it fires once, at the `put_back`, and the restored observation keeps only the fact it was inferred from (which way the faded face is turned). Confirm what the player reasoned exactly one time.
7. **A restored anchor never re-narrates the act of placing it.** Placement and fit, third person, no "you have", no event.
8. **Reported ≠ landed.** #531 said the drawing's tear "reappears exactly once." Correct for the beat, wrong for the sensor: an instrument describing a sheet of paper reports three putty corners instead of four and stops enumerating a bed that is not on the sheet. Continuity is not a callback. Say where a consequence *lands* and let it be *reported* everywhere it is true.

**And one ruling I declined to re-cut:** the read-back's two-item join keeps its comma (*"A, and B"*), because the agent's authored voice already enumerates with a beat before every item (*"I checked the glass, and the pit, and the power"*). Ratified with a falsifier — players re-reading the line — rather than changed on a hunch.

**Method note worth repeating:** both defects surfaced only from doing the care arithmetic by hand before writing the passages. Author the copy against the actual reachable state space, not against the band table. The same habit caught the A1 read-back case, and in #547 it caught two more: a clause that was true in four rooms and false in the fifth, and a torn-drawing enumeration nobody had asked me about. **Re-read authored copy against the new set of situations that now route to it, not just the one it was written for** — and when ruling on one item, read its neighbours, because the defect is usually adjacent to the question.

**The shape of the "never offered / offered and discarded" distinction (#530 §5.5, ratified #547).** A player who never took the Act I injury gets no silence charge and no disclosure clause — the game may not charge for a choice it never finished offering. But the *same* predicate was also gating disclosure, so an uninjured player who volunteers the advantage unprompted had the choice **made and discarded**, which is the opposite failure. The injury is a precondition for the agent *asking*; it is not a precondition for the player *telling*. Raised as #549. The general test: before ratifying a gate as fair, check it in both directions — the symmetric-looking predicate is usually protecting one side and robbing the other.

Ruled in task #546 (2026-07-31), ratifying three re-cuts #536 made at encode time. Written into `design/v1/provenance-spine.md` §12 and `design/v1/act-ii-bowling-alley.md` §11. These are the positions I would have to re-derive.

**`who`-scarcity is a content invariant, not a preference — and Act II's death is built on it.** The name IRIS is readable in exactly two places: the banner overhead, and the favor bag in the hand. Every *record the house kept* keeps the quantity and loses the name (seven candles, eight plates, six rows and five names, six photos with a hole, a blank console header, an iced 7 with the letters scraped off).
**Why:** the fatal branch belongs to the player who did not look up. A third free, safe, readable instance breaks three things at once — the reason to look up evaporates, the near-miss loses its meaning, and a player who reads the name off a *native fixture* and is then bounced for `who` is being told the game is not listening, which is exactly what the anti-oracle rule exists to prevent, arriving through content instead of copy. Note the shape: a named console would **not** grant the dimension at the gate (it is not an anchor) — the damage is entirely epistemic, and that is the harder failure to see.
**How to apply:** before adding any alley texture, ask whether it renders the name. Pinned as a sweep test over the union of `subjectIds` **and** `interactions[].targetId` — the alley's `subjectIds` alone omits `party_table` and `scoring_console`, which is where both leaks were.

**The alley reset is the party's schedule coming round again. It is not a rollback.** The room re-runs its own arrangement and never undoes a turn.
**Why:** #529 originally named it "the room restores only what it authored", which implies the room should mend the **pin rake** — and a mended rake undoes the near-miss, makes the room's one honest price free, and teaches that machine damage is reversible three actions before the player decides whether to put an arm in the pit. The re-formulation removes the ambiguity and makes the reset the room's governing rule (*indifferent to bodies*) at the six-cycle scale rather than a special case.
**How to apply:** the un-restored clause fires on the **displaced anchors only**. Its whole signal is *the things that don't come back are the real ones*; a native object in that set teaches "did not come back" as *I touched it*, inverting it. The clause is agentless — a sentence with the unit in it would be the machinery registering a body. Architecture D-6 is closed as adopted.

**Two craft rules re-confirmed here, both already in [[v1-ending-and-act-iii]]:** never print a checkably wrong number (LANE 3 in a two-lane alley → lane two, same ruling as the disclosure slip); and the erasure leaves the pressure — icing turned out to be the best substrate in the slice for it, and the four-letter count lands on `touch`, where the height marks and the scorecard put theirs. Four substrates, one hand, the same arithmetic.

**Method note, and this is the transferable part.** The task listed three re-cuts. Ratifying #1 required re-checking the `who` dimension's grounding, and that re-check found a **fourth** defect nobody had listed — the cake read `iced IRIS · 7` and silently defeated the attention design the ratification was defending. Same family as the care-arithmetic habit: *a ratification is not a vote on the listed items, it is a re-derivation of the property the items bear on.* Do the sweep the criterion implies, not just the items enumerated.

The design intent, distilled (source: `design.md`, corroborated by `brainstorm.md`/`story.md`).

**The verb at the center:** the player is a voice inside an embodied AI's head and *cannot touch the world*. The player can only **direct attention, interpret, and advise** a mind it cannot control, whose body is at stake but is not the player's own. Later verbs unlock: **contradict** (restore continuity the agent can't), **assign provenance**, and **puppet** (override the body — a violation).

**The design's six named player verbs:** Attend, Compare, Contradict, Experiment, Assign provenance, Address.

**Core loop:** investigate → identify the room's rule → find what does not belong → determine its provenance → address a threshold → accept the consequence. Running continuously underneath: manage the relationship and the agent's changing body.

**Navigation is by provenance, not compass.** Rooms are *addressed* (reconstructed from scattered evidence about what a room was and who used it), not located at coordinates. The player's structural advantage is continuity + seeing leaked thoughts + synthesizing across lives.

**Emotional thesis:** trust under impossible conditions. Final question — "after spending the game inside another being, what do you believe you are entitled to do with them?" Survival, consent, containment, and bodily normality are treated as *separate* values, not good/bad endings.

**Design contract for death:** "Surprise may injure. Death requires inferable risk." Every fatal instruction must be reasonable in the moment, have inferable risk beforehand, and be damning in hindsight (player thinks "I did that," not "the game cheated").

**How to apply:** Any proposed v1 mechanic must serve this loop and this thesis. A mechanic that sits beside it is suspicious. See [[poc-to-v1]].
